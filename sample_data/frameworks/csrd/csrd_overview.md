# Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) - Overview

## Introduction

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) is a European Union directive that significantly expands sustainability reporting requirements for companies. It replaces the Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD) and introduces the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS).

## Key Requirements

### Scope of Application

**Large Companies** (meeting 2 of 3 criteria):
- Balance sheet total: €25 million
- Net turnover: €50 million  
- Average employees: 250

**Listed SMEs** (meeting 2 of 3 criteria):
- Balance sheet total: €4 million
- Net turnover: €8 million
- Average employees: 10

**Non-EU Companies**: €150 million EU turnover threshold

### Implementation Timeline

- **2025**: Large companies already subject to NFRD
- **2026**: Other large companies  
- **2027**: Listed SMEs
- **2028**: Non-EU companies

## European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)

### Cross-cutting Standards
- **ESRS 1**: General requirements
- **ESRS 2**: General disclosures

### Environmental Standards
- **ESRS E1**: Climate change
- **ESRS E2**: Pollution
- **ESRS E3**: Water and marine resources
- **ESRS E4**: Biodiversity and ecosystems
- **ESRS E5**: Resource use and circular economy

### Social Standards
- **ESRS S1**: Own workforce
- **ESRS S2**: Workers in the value chain
- **ESRS S3**: Affected communities
- **ESRS S4**: Consumers and end-users

### Governance Standards
- **ESRS G1**: Business conduct

## Double Materiality Assessment

Companies must assess both:

### Impact Materiality
- Positive and negative impacts on people and environment
- Actual and potential impacts
- Short, medium, and long-term impacts

### Financial Materiality  
- Sustainability matters creating financial risks or opportunities
- Effects on financial position, performance, cash flows, access to finance

## Key Disclosure Requirements

### Strategy and Business Model
- Business model description
- Strategy for sustainability matters
- Resilience of strategy and business model

### Governance
- Role of governance bodies
- Integration into risk management
- Due diligence processes

### Impact, Risk and Opportunity Management
- Processes to identify and assess impacts, risks, opportunities
- Policies and actions
- Tracking effectiveness

### Metrics and Targets
- Targets related to material impacts, risks, opportunities
- Progress against targets
- Key performance indicators

## Climate Change Disclosures (ESRS E1)

### Governance
- Oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities
- Management's role in assessment and management

### Strategy
- Climate-related risks and opportunities
- Resilience of strategy (scenario analysis)
- Transition plan

### Impact, Risk and Opportunity Management
- Processes for identifying and assessing climate-related risks
- Risk management integration

### Metrics and Targets
- Greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1, 2, 3)
- Climate-related targets
- Energy consumption and energy mix
- Progress against targets

## Compliance Considerations

### Assurance Requirements
- Limited assurance initially
- Reasonable assurance for certain indicators by 2028

### Digital Reporting
- Reports must be machine-readable (XHTML format)
- Tagging requirements for data extraction

### Comparability
- Standardized disclosure requirements
- Consistent metrics across companies

## Integration with Other Frameworks

### EU Taxonomy Alignment
- Assessment against EU Taxonomy criteria
- Revenue, CapEx, OpEx alignment disclosure

### Other Standards Compatibility
- Builds on existing frameworks (GRI, SASB, TCFD)
- Allows use of other standards if requirements met

## Penalties and Enforcement

### Member State Implementation
- Administrative penalties
- Publication of violations
- Corrective measures

### Potential Sanctions
- Financial penalties
- Prohibition of profit distribution
- Director disqualification

## Benefits and Challenges

### Benefits
- Enhanced transparency and accountability
- Better access to sustainable finance
- Competitive advantage through sustainability leadership
- Improved risk management

### Challenges
- Significant compliance costs
- Data collection and management complexity
- Need for new skills and capabilities
- Third-party assurance requirements

## Getting Started

### 1. Materiality Assessment
- Conduct double materiality assessment
- Identify material sustainability matters
- Prioritize disclosure requirements

### 2. Gap Analysis
- Compare current reporting against ESRS requirements
- Identify data collection needs
- Assess systems and processes

### 3. Implementation Planning
- Develop implementation roadmap
- Assign responsibilities
- Plan system and process changes

### 4. Stakeholder Engagement
- Engage with stakeholders on material matters
- Incorporate stakeholder perspectives
- Communicate preparation progress
